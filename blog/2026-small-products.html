为什么我不建议你从"自己的想法"开始Why I Don't Recommend Starting From "My Own Idea"

"我想做个 XX"是所有产品失败故事的起点。2025 年我记录过自己 6 个"从零想出来"的产品，结果是：3 个没人用，2 个有人用但不付钱，1 个做到月入 ¥400 就停了。而我后来做的那个月入 ¥7,260 的简历优化器，根本不是我想出来的，是客户用钱包投出来的。 "I want to build an X" is where every failed product begins. In 2025 I tracked six products I invented from scratch: three nobody used, two were used but not paid for, and one plateaued at ¥400 a month. The résumé optimizer that now earns ¥7,260 a month wasn't my idea at all — customers elected it with their wallets.

所以这篇文章不讲"怎么产生一个酷想法"，而是直接给 10 个已经被验证过的小产品：每个都写清楚谁会用、用什么技术、我或别人实际花了多久做完、能收多少钱、以及最大的坑是什么。你要做的是挑一个抄，而不是从空白页开始。 So this article isn't about generating a clever idea. It gives you ten already-validated micro-products: who uses them, what tech they need, how long it actually took, what they can charge, and the biggest trap in each. Your job is to pick one and copy it, not to start from a blank page.

下面这 10 个都满足同三个条件：一个人 2 周内能做出来、解决的问题足够具体（有人现在就在用 Excel 或微信手工做）、结果可以被一眼验收（对完就完事，不用解释）。All ten share three properties: one person can build it in under two weeks, the problem is specific enough (people are doing it by hand in Excel or WeChat right now), and the result is checkable at a glance — done is done, nothing to explain.

先搞清楚：什么叫"够小"First: What "Small Enough" Actually Means

"小"不是功能少，而是只有一个人能同时搞定的复杂度。我用三条判断，任何一条不满足，这个产品对新手就太大了： "Small" doesn't mean few features. It means a complexity one person can hold in their head at once. I use three tests; fail any one and the product is too big for a beginner:

① 输入是什么1. What's the input? 一份文件 / 一段文字 / 一个表格A file, a block of text, a spreadsheet 一堆要连起来的数据源Several data sources to join
② 输出是什么2. What's the output? 一页表格 / 一段带标记的文本 / 一张图One table, one annotated text, one image 一个要长期维护的系统A system you must maintain forever
③ 怎么算做对了3. How do you know it's right? 一眼能看出来Visible at a glance 要懂业务才能判断Needs domain expertise to judge

第三条最容易被忽略，也最致命。如果你自己都不能一眼看出结果对不对，客户也看不出——他就不敢付第二次钱。我的定价页因此都会写一句"不满意可退款"，因为我知道有些东西我判断不了，只能靠客户来判。 The third test gets skipped most and is the most dangerous. If you can't tell at a glance whether the output is right, the client can't either — and won't pay a second time. That's why my pricing pages all say "refund if you're not satisfied": some things I can't judge, so the client has to.

抄法示范 1：合同关键条款提取Copy Example 1: Contract Clause Extractor

需求来自一个做外贸的朋友：他每次签合同要人工找违约金、付款节奏、知识产权归属、管辖法院这 4 项，翻 30 页 PDF 要 40 分钟，还经常漏。他一年签 200 多份，这就是一个真实存在、且有人愿意花钱的 40 分钟。 The request came from a friend in foreign trade: for every contract he manually located the penalty clause, payment schedule, IP ownership, and jurisdiction — forty minutes of scrolling through 30-page PDFs, and he often missed one. He signs over 200 contracts a year, so that's a real forty minutes that someone will pay to get back.

输出长什么样（先定这个）Define the output first

固定 4 行，每行 3 段：条款原文（引用）、风险等级（高/中/低）、建议动作。输出格式固定，用户才有习惯。不要做成"AI 帮你分析合同"——那是聊天，做不成产品。Always four rows, three fields each: the clause quoted verbatim, a risk level (high/medium/low), and a suggested action. A fixed output shape is what creates a habit. Don't ship "AI analyses your contract" — that's a chat feature, not a product.

技术上的关键一步：切分The one technical crux: chunking

整份合同直接丢给模型会漏（长文档被读漏是普遍现象）。正确做法是先按章节切成块，再每块问 4 个问题，最后合并。切分用"章节标题 + 段落开头"做锚点，比按固定字数切靠谱得多——按字数切会把一个条款劈成两半。Feeding the whole contract to the model loses content — long-document truncation is universal. The fix: split into sections first, ask the four questions per section, then merge. Anchor chunks on headings and paragraph openings rather than fixed character counts, which slice a single clause in half.

定价与交付Price and deliver

我给他做的是 ¥299/份，当天出结果，每份附一个"我人工核对了这 4 项"的声明。他第一个月用了 9 份，之后改成了 ¥2,999/年的包月。这个产品最后变成了我「AI 接单」里最稳定的一类收入。I charged ¥299 per contract, same-day delivery, each with a note saying "I verified these four items by hand". He used nine in the first month and then moved to ¥2,999/year. It became the steadiest line in my service business.

这个产品最大的坑The biggest trap in this one

法律责任。合同分析结果如果出了错，谁负责？我的做法是明写免责：工具做初筛和提示，最终判断由使用者负责，并在输出里标注"引用自第 X 页，可回原文核对"。这一条让我的客户放心，也让这件事从"AI 瞎说"变成"AI 帮你快速定位"。Liability. If the analysis is wrong, who is responsible? My answer: an explicit disclaimer that the tool pre-screens and flags, and the human decides, plus every quote labelled "from page X — go verify". That single line reassures clients and turns "AI made something up" into "AI found it fast".

抄法示范 2：电商图批量改尺寸与命名Copy Example 2: Bulk Product-Image Resizing

这个的逻辑最简单，但需求最密集：一个淘宝卖家每周上新 40 件商品，每件要出 5 个尺寸（主图、详情图、缩略图……）并按平台规则重命名。他每周花 3 小时在拖鼠标。 The logic here is the simplest, but the pain is the most repetitive: a Taobao seller lists 40 products a week, each needing five sizes (hero, detail, thumbnail…) renamed to platform rules. He spends three hours a week dragging the mouse.

这个产品的技术含量最低，但有三个细节决定它是"能用"还是"没人用"：第一，命名规则要能自己配（不同平台不一样）；第二，压缩后画质不能糊；第三，要给一张对照表让他确认，别直接覆盖原图。 The technical depth is the lowest, but three details separate "usable" from "ignored": naming rules must be configurable (every platform differs), compression must not blur the image, and you must show a before/after table rather than overwriting originals.

定价我做的是 ¥99/月不限量，因为处理成本几乎为零（一次上传，几分钟跑完）。真正贵的是"帮你把第一次的规则配好"，那部分是一次性 ¥300 的设置费。这个结构后来被我的其他工具照抄：按月收低固定费，把一次性设置和规则定制单独收费。 I charged ¥99/month unlimited, because processing costs almost nothing. The expensive part is configuring the rules correctly the first time, so that's a one-off ¥300 setup fee. I've reused that structure ever since: a low flat monthly fee, with setup and custom rules priced separately.

抄法示范 3：岗位匹配度打分Copy Example 3: Job-Match Scorecard

求职者投完简历不知道差在哪。这个产品做两件事：把简历和岗位描述逐条对比，输出 5 个匹配项 + 3 个差距项 + 3 条具体改写建议。难点在于"差距项"要具体到句子。 Job seekers send résumés and never learn why they were ignored. This one compares a résumé against a job description line by line and outputs 5 matches, 3 gaps, and 3 concrete rewrite suggestions. The hard part is making each gap specific to a sentence.

我的做法是把岗位描述先拆成"硬性要求 / 加分项 / 关键词"三栏，再逐条去简历里找证据，找不到就是差距，找到就引用原文。输出里必须带原文引用，因为求职者要拿这个去改简历，他需要知道"我漏了哪句"。 My method: split the job description into "required / nice-to-have / keywords", then hunt for evidence for each line in the résumé — no evidence means a gap; found evidence means quote it. The output must quote the original, because the candidate needs to know which sentence to rewrite.

定价 ¥19/次，比订阅低很多，因为这是"一次性焦虑"而不是"日常需求"。低单价高频次是这个模式的特点：我的用户里 34% 一个月用了 3 次以上。 Priced at ¥19 per run, well below a subscription, because this is one-off anxiety rather than a daily need. Low price, high frequency is the signature of this model: 34% of my users ran it more than three times in a month.

If you want to build something like these, the cheapest order is always: serve twenty people by hand, then write code.

用 4 个问题挑出你的那一个Pick Your One With Four Questions

① 你有 3 个月没跟人说过话的领域吗？1. Is there a field you could talk about for 3 hours straight?

有，就选那个领域的产品。合同提取对应外贸和 HR，图片处理对应电商，打分对应招聘。你对领域的理解，比你对技术的理解更值钱——技术是通用能力，领域知识才是别人抄不走的。If yes, pick from that field. Clause extraction maps to trade and HR, image tooling to e-commerce, scorecards to recruitment. Your domain knowledge is worth more than your technical knowledge — technical skill is generic; knowing a field is what nobody can copy.

② 你身边有人正在手工做这件事吗？2. Does someone you know do this by hand right now?

这是最重要的一个问题。手工做意味着：痛是真实的、频率是可算的、验收标准是现成的（看他现在怎么做）。找不到这样的人，就别做这个产品——那说明痛点还没被验证过，你只是看别人抱怨而已。This is the most important question. Doing it by hand means the pain is real, the frequency is countable, and the acceptance criteria already exist — watch how they do it today. If you can't find that person, don't build it: the pain is unvalidated and all you have is other people's complaints.

③ 你能不能在 7 天内做出一个"能给人看"的版本？3. Can you have a demonstrable version in 7 days?

"能给人看"的意思是：一个真实的人，把真实的东西丢进去，拿到一个能用的结果。页面丑一点没关系，能跑通输入输出闭环就行。做不到，说明技术选错了，不是你不行。"Demonstrable" means: a real person puts a real thing in and gets a usable result out. Ugly pages are fine — the input-output loop just has to close. If you can't, the stack choice is wrong, not you.

④ 你能接受它可能一辈子只赚 ¥3,000 吗？4. Can you accept it might never earn more than ¥3,000 a month?

大部分小产品的天花板就是月入几千块。我自己的模板包每月稳定在 ¥3,100，我会用它交房租，但它从没让我发财。接受这件事，你才能做出好东西；不接受，你会不停换项目，然后什么都没做完。Most micro-products cap out in the low thousands per month. My own template pack sits steadily at ¥3,100 — it pays rent but never made me rich. Accept that and you can build something good; refuse it and you'll keep switching projects and finish nothing.

抄之前，先确认这 3 件事Before You Copy: Confirm These Three Things

  1. 你的第一个客户不能是陌生人。从你那个"手工做这件事"的人开始。第一个客户来自陌生人时，你大概率会做出他不要的东西，然后归咎于市场。Your first client can't be a stranger. Start with the person who does it by hand. When your first client is a stranger, you will probably build what they don't want and blame the market.
  2. 先手工交付 5 单，再写代码。这 5 单会告诉你：输入长什么样、输出到什么程度算够、愿意付多少钱。你会发现真正该做的功能，跟你以为的根本不是一回事。Deliver five jobs by hand before you write code. Those five tell you what the inputs really look like, what "good enough" means, and what people will pay. The real feature list usually isn't the one you imagined.
  3. 确认它不依赖某个会收费或改规则的第三方。抓取类、平台 API 类产品最容易踩坑：平台一改规则或者一封号，你的收入当场归零。抄之前先查一遍依赖。Check it doesn't depend on a third party that can charge you or change the rules. Scraping and platform-API products are the most fragile: when the rules change or the account gets banned, your revenue is zero that day. Check dependencies before you copy.
抄作业不丢人。丢人的是花三个月做出一个没人要的东西，然后归咎于"AI 不行"。 Copying isn't shameful. Spending three months building something nobody wanted — and blaming AI — is.
最后的建议Final advice

如果只能选一个，我推荐第 4 个（会议录音转待办）——不是因为它最赚钱，而是因为它你自己就是用户。做自己的产品时，每个决定都不用在脑子里演"客户会想要吗"，因为你就是客户。我做这个产品时，第一版只有 40 分钟，因为我自己每天都在用。做 3 周之后我才知道，真正被用得最多的是"只提取有明确负责人和截止日期的待办"这一条——这件事没有任何用户告诉过我，是我自己用了 3 周发现的。If you can only pick one, take #4 (meeting audio to to-dos) — not because it earns most, but because you are the user. Building for yourself means you never have to imagine "would a client want this?", because you are the client. My first version took 40 minutes. After three weeks of daily use I learned that the feature people actually used was "only extract action items with a named owner and a due date" — something no user ever told me. Three weeks of using it myself found that.
