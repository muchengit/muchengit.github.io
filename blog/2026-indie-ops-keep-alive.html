2026.10.16 — 上线之后才刚开始，以及一份每周 30 分钟的运维清单2026.10.16 — What happens after launch, and a 30-minute weekly ops checklist

上线那一刻，事情才刚开始The Moment You Launch, Things Begin

我做过 3 个上线的小产品。第 1 个活了下来，第 2 个在第 4 个月悄悄挂了（我 11 天后才从朋友那里听说），第 3 个现在每月有 3800 个用户付费用户——不是收入，是"还在用的人"。三个产品里，我花在上线后运维的时间，超过了写代码的时间。 I've shipped three small products. The first survived, the second went down silently in month four (I heard about it 11 days later, from a friend), and the third now has 3,800 people actively using it — not revenue, people still using it. Across all three, the time I spent on post-launch operations exceeded the time I spent writing code.

这和大众的想象不一样。做产品的那两周是 highs：发布、发朋友圈、收到第一笔付款。运维的那几个月是 lows：账单突然翻倍、用户来骂、某个第三方 API 半夜改了行为、数据库磁盘满。而 lows 会杀死 highs。 This doesn't match the popular image. The two weeks of building are highs: launch, announcements, first payment. The months of operations are lows: a bill that doubles overnight, an angry user, a third-party API changing behavior at 3am, a full disk. And lows kill highs.

AI 产品的运维和普通软件有一个本质区别：你的主要成本不在服务器，在模型调用，而模型调用的账单波动比流量大得多。一个周末的自然流量高峰，配上一个没有限额设置的接口，可能一周烧掉两个月利润。所以这篇的重点不只是"别挂了"，更是"别被账单杀死"。AI product operations differ from ordinary software in one essential way: your main cost isn't the server, it's model calls — and model bills swing far more than traffic does.A weekend traffic spike plus an endpoint with no spending cap can burn two months of profit in a week. So this piece is as much about "don't get killed by the bill" as "don't go down."

用户最多、成本第二高，但每用户成本最低——因为它做了两件事：用小模型处理简单请求，只有复杂请求才升级到大模型；以及对同一份内容做缓存。这两个动作把它的模型成本从每用户 $0.41 压到了 $0.19，降幅 54%，一个月省了 $98。

The highest-leverage cost optimization in any AI product is "don't use a big model for a small model's job."

成本失控的三个真实案例Three Real Cost Blowups

案例一：周末流量高峰，一周烧掉 $1,340Case 1: A Weekend Traffic Spike Cost $1,340

2026 年 3 月，一个工具被 Reddit 帖子带火，周六到周日来了约 40 倍平时流量。那个周末我收到 11 封"你的服务不可用"的邮件，还有一封 Stripe 扣款 $1,340 的通知。根因：我从来没有设置过消费上限，而且没有做并发限制——每个请求都直接调了一次付费 API。 In March 2026 a tool took off from a Reddit thread and took roughly 40× normal traffic over the weekend. I got 11 "service unavailable" emails and a Stripe charge for $1,340. Root cause: I had never set a spending cap, and there was no rate limiting — every request called a paid API directly.

案例二：重试没有上限，一次超时花了 $46Case 2: Unbounded Retries Cost $46 in One Request

同一个产品的另一次事故。某个第三方 API 超时了，AI 帮我写的重试逻辑没有设置最大重试次数，于是一个请求重试了 200 次，每次都计费。我是在看账单时发现的。这次损失小，因为我第二天就加了上限，但如果没有看账单，可能会再烧掉几百块。 Another incident in the same product. A third-party API timed out, and the retry logic AI had written had no maximum retry count, so one request retried 200 times, each one billed. I found it by reading the bill. The loss was small because I added a cap the next day — but without that bill, it could have run for hundreds more.

案例三：一个用户的用量吃掉了全部利润Case 3: One User Ate the Entire Margin

产品 C 有个企业客户，把我们的周报功能接进了他们自己的流程，每天跑 800 次。一个月下来，这个客户的模型账单是 $312，而他付给我们的钱是 $29。我是在做每用户成本分析时才发现的——如果不做那张表，这个客户会一直每个月让我亏 $283。 Product C had an enterprise client who plugged our weekly report into their own workflow and ran it 800 times a day. That client's model bill was $312 for the month, against $29 paid to us. I found it while building the per-user cost table — without that table, this client would have cost me $283 every month indefinitely.

三个案例的共同解法The Common Fix Across All Three

它们都不是"AI 会不会出错"的问题，是失控没有上限的问题。三个解法，加起来大概 40 行代码：
① 消费上限：在模型服务商后台设月度预算告警和硬上限
② 重试上限：任何重试逻辑必须有 max_retries 参数，默认不超过 3
③ 每用户配额：超过配额的请求直接拒绝，不要"先跑着看看"

这三行配置，救过的钱比我一整年的订阅费都多。 None of these was about "AI being wrong" — they were about runaway processes with no ceiling. Three fixes, about 40 lines of configuration in total:
① Spend caps: monthly budget alerts and a hard limit in the model provider's console
② Retry caps: every retry path gets a max_retries parameter, defaulting to 3
③ Per-user quotas: requests over quota get rejected, not "let's run it and see"

Those three settings have saved more money than a full year of my subscriptions.

备份：3-2-1 原则在个人项目怎么落地Backups: Making the 3-2-1 Rule Work for a One-Person Project

经典的 3-2-1 原则是：3 份副本，2 种介质，1 份异地。听起来很重，但个人项目可以做到很轻。关键是理解要防的是什么：不是"服务器被砸了"这种小概率事件，而是"我自己手滑删了"和"我忘了它坏了"这两种高概率事件。 The classic 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite.That sounds heavy, but a one-person product can do it lightly. The key is understanding what you're defending against — not "the server gets destroyed" (rare), but "I deleted it by accident" and "I forgot it was broken" (common). 

# 每天凌晨 3 点，全量导出数据库并加密
0 3 * * * /opt/backup/run.sh && curl -s -X POST "$ALERT_WEBHOOK" -d "备份完成"

# 备份脚本的关键三步：导出、加密、送走
set -euo pipefail
STAMP=$(date +%Y%m%d-%H%M)
mkdir -p /tmp/bk

# 1) 导出成可读格式，而不是只做二进制快照
pg_dump "$DATABASE_URL" | gzip > /tmp/bk/db-$STAMP.sql.gz

# 2) 用独立密钥加密，密钥放在环境变量里，不进代码库
openssl enc -aes-256-cbc -pbkdf2 -iter 100000 \
  -in /tmp/bk/db-$STAMP.sql.gz -out /tmp/bk/db-$STAMP.sql.gz.enc \
  -pass env:"BACKUP_KEY"

# 3) 送到异地（对象存储按月几分钱）
aws s3 cp /tmp/bk/db-$STAMP.sql.gz.enc \
  "s3://my-backups/db-$STAMP.sql.gz.enc" --storage-class STANDARD_IA

# 4) 保留最近 30 天，其余自动清理
find /tmp/bk -type f -mtime +30 -delete
没恢复过的备份，不算备份A Backup You Have Never Restored Is Not a Backup

这是运维里最反直觉的一条。备份文件存在硬盘上，不代表你能恢复它——密钥可能变了、格式可能不兼容、脚本可能从第一天起就是坏的。每个月挑一个旧备份，真的在另一台机器上恢复一次，哪怕只是导入一个空库确认能打开。

我第一次做恢复测试是在上线第 5 个月，脚本少写了一个参数，直接失败了。修好之后我才知道，之前 5 个月的"备份"全是不能用的文件。你不会希望知道这件事是在用户投诉的那天。 This is the most counterintuitive rule in operations. A backup file existing on disk doesn't mean you can restore it — the key may have changed, the format may be incompatible, the script may have been broken since day one. Once a month, pick an old backup and actually restore it somewhere else, even if it's just importing into an empty database to confirm it opens.

My first restore test was in month five, and the script was missing a parameter and failed immediately. Fixing it is how I learned that five months of "backups" were unusable files. You'd rather not learn that on the day a customer complains.

#!/bin/bash
SPEND=$(curl -s "$BILLING_API" | jq -r '.today_usd')
AVG=$(curl -s "$STATS_API" | jq -r '.avg_daily_usd')
DISK=$(df -h / | awk 'NR==2{print $5}' | tr -d '%')

# 花钱超了，或者磁盘快满了，都立刻发消息
if (( $(echo "$SPEND > $AVG * 2" | bc -l) )); then
  send_alert "今天模型花费 $SPEND 美元，是日均的 2 倍以上，请检查"
fi
if (( DISK > 80 )); then
  send_alert "磁盘已用 $DISK%，请清理日志"
fi
echo "ok spend=$SPEND disk=$DISK%"

自建监控，还是用托管平台自带的Build Your Own Monitoring or Use What the Platform Gives You

用平台自带的（Vercel / Railway / Cloudflare）What the Platform Gives You

适合：部署在托管平台上的应用。

优势：零配置就有基础指标、日志和告警；不用自己运维监控本身；免费额度通常够小产品用。
代价：只有平台层指标，看不到"用户能不能真正完成核心功能"；平台换了你得重配。

我的用法：产品 A 和 B 用平台自带的，5 分钟就配好了。 Good for: apps hosted on a managed platform.

Upside: baseline metrics, logs, and alerts with zero setup; you don't operate the monitoring itself; free tiers usually suffice.
Cost: platform-level metrics only — you can't see whether users can actually complete the core flow; reconfiguring if you switch platforms.

My use: products A and B use the built-ins, configured in five minutes.

自建（自己写脚本 + 第三方探活）Build It Yourself (Scripts + Third-Party Probes)

适合：自托管在 VPS 上的服务，或者需要验证"核心流程真的能用"的产品。

优势：能测真实业务逻辑，而不只是端口活不活；跨平台不迁移。
代价：要多花 1 到 2 小时搭建；自己写的东西自己也会挂。

我的用法：产品 C 用自建，因为它的核心流程必须真的跑一次模型调用才算数。 Good for: services on your own VPS, or anything where "the core flow really works" matters.

Upside: tests real business logic, not just a live port; no migration cost.
Cost: 1–2 hours to build; the things you write can also break.

My use: product C is self-built, because its core flow only counts if it really makes a model call.

两者的关键区别是测什么：平台监控告诉你"进程还活着"，自建脚本能告诉你"用户真的能完成核心操作"。后者才是唯一能提前发现"AI 功能挂了"的方式——因为模型接口的故障往往不会让进程退出，只是让结果变空。 The real difference is what you test. Platform monitoring tells you "the process is alive"; your own script tells you "a user can actually finish the core task." Only the latter catches "the AI feature broke" early — because a model API failure usually doesn't kill the process, it just makes the results empty.

数据导出：用户有权带走自己的东西Data Export: Users Deserve to Take Their Data With Them

这一条跟技术无关，跟信任有关。不管你的产品做多大，给用户一个"一键导出全部数据"的按钮，成本很低，回报很高。我 2026 年 4 月加上这个功能后，主动流失（取消订阅后不再使用）下降了 9 个百分点——虽然这不能算严谨的因果，但同期没有任何其他改动。 This one isn't about technology, it's about trust. Whatever the size of your product, a one-click "export everything" button is cheap to build and pays back well.When I added it in April 2026, voluntary churn dropped 9 percentage points — not rigorous causation, but nothing else changed in that period.

顺带的一个好处An Unexpected Bonus

有了导出功能，你就顺便有了自己数据的主副本。每季度导出一次放在本地，既是给用户的便利，也是你自己的备份。我现在的做法是：用户点导出时同时在我的备份桶里留一份——这比任何定时任务都可靠，因为它有人触发。 The export feature gives you a primary copy of your own data as a side effect. Exporting once a quarter to local storage is both a user courtesy and your own backup. My current approach: when a user exports, I also keep a copy in my backup bucket — more reliable than any scheduled job, because a human triggers it.

每周 30 分钟的运维清单The 30-Minute Weekly Ops Checklist

把这五件事固定在每周同一天，比如周六上午。30 分钟，一个月 2 小时。这是我维护三个产品的全部时间投入。 Fix these five on the same day each week — Saturday morning works. Thirty minutes, two hours a month. That's my entire time budget for running three products.

看账单（5 分钟）Read the Bill (5 min)

对比本周和上周的模型花费。涨超过 50% 就查一下是什么触发的；跌超过 50% 也查——可能是功能挂了没人调用。Compare this week's model spend to last week's. Up more than 50%, find out what drove it. Down more than 50%, find out too — the feature may be broken and nobody's calling it.

看核心功能是否真的能用（5 分钟）Test That the Core Feature Works (5 min)

自己用一遍主流程，不是看监控面板，是真的做一次：上传一张图、生成一段报告、发一封邮件。只有你自己走过一遍，才知道用户那边是什么感觉。Walk the main flow yourself — not a dashboard, an actual use: upload an image, generate a report, send an email. Only walking it yourself tells you what it feels like on the user's side.

看错误日志（8 分钟）Skim the Error Logs (8 min)

只看你自己埋的那类错误：模型返回空、返回格式不对、超时。重复出现的错误要么修，要么加进"已知问题"清单告诉用户。Only the errors you instrumented yourself: empty model responses, malformed output, timeouts. A recurring error gets fixed, or gets added to a "known issues" list you tell users about.

恢复一次备份（7 分钟）Restore One Backup (7 min)

每月一次，挑一个 7 天前的备份真的恢复出来。这件事很枯燥，但它是你唯一能提前知道"备份能不能用"的方式。Once a month, restore a backup from seven days ago. It's boring, and it's the only way to know in advance whether your backups work.

看一眼数字（5 分钟）Look at the Numbers (5 min)

活跃用户、每用户成本、付费转化。三个数字，一眼看出产品是在变好还是在变差。如果连续两周活跃用户在掉，先别加功能，先看是不是哪里坏了。Active users, cost per user, conversion. Three numbers that show at a glance whether things are improving.If active users drop two weeks running, don't add features — check whether something broke first.

这 30 分钟的价值不在于"发现问题"，在于让你有底气判断什么时候该停。运维做得好的人，关掉一个产品时不会犹豫——因为数据摆在那里。 The value of those 30 minutes isn't catching problems — it's being able to judge when to stop. People who run operations well can shut a product down without hesitation, because the numbers are right there.

什么时候该关掉它When to Shut It Down

我的第 2 个产品——那个白屏 11 天没人知道的——我在它还有 40 个活跃用户的时候关掉了。听起来可惜，但那 40 个人没有一个人为它付过钱，而它每月要花我 $23。继续做一件没人要的事，不是坚持，是惯性。 I shut down my second product — the one that was down for 11 days — while it still had 40 active users. That sounds wasteful, but not one of those 40 was paying, and it cost me $23 a month. Continuing something nobody wants isn't persistence, it's inertia.

我的关摊标准有三条，满足两条就关： I have three shutdown criteria. Two out of three means shut it down:

  • 连续 3 个月付费用户零增长，且期间做过至少一次真正的改进（不是改文案）。Paid users flat for 3 consecutive months, during which you shipped at least one real improvement (not a copy tweak).
  • 每个付费用户的毛利为负，或者成本超过收入的 70%。Gross margin per paying user is negative, or cost exceeds 70% of revenue.
  • 你自己一个月内没有主动打开过它——不是因为忙，是因为不想用。You haven't opened it yourself in a month — not because you're busy, but because you don't want to.

第三条最主观，也最准。它对应的其实是另一个问题：你做这个产品是因为用户需要，还是因为你舍不得已经投进去的时间？后者不叫坚持，叫沉没成本。 The third is the most subjective and the most accurate. It maps to a different question: did you build this because users need it, or because you can't bear to waste the time already spent?The second one isn't persistence; it's sunk cost.

如果这份清单只让你记住一件事，记这条：设上限。消费上限、重试上限、每用户配额、磁盘告警——四行配置，救过的钱比我做产品赚过的钱都多。 If this checklist leaves you with one thing, make it this: set ceilings. Spend caps, retry caps, per-user quotas, disk alerts — four settings that have saved me more money than my products ever earned.

至于"一个人的产品要怎么活下去"这个问题，我的答案有点扫兴：大部分人应该只维护一个。三个产品听起来很勤奋，实际上意味着三个都维护不好。我现在的状态是：一个认真做、认真运维、一个半死不活地挂着、一个已经关了。听起来不体面，但数字是对的。 As for "how does a one-person product survive," my answer is a bit deflating: most people should maintain exactly one.Three products sounds diligent; it really means three that are all half-maintained. Where I am now: one built properly and maintained properly, one limping along, one closed. It doesn't sound impressive, but the numbers are honest.
